Why RedHatAI: Qwen publishes only BF16 (55.6 GB) and FP8 (30.9 GB) for Qwen3.8-27B; neither fits 24 GB, and there is
no official Int4/3-bit/mixed release, so nothing official sits near 12 GB. The nearest-size vLLM checkpoint,
ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-27B-3Bit-GSQ (11.8 GB W3A16), needs a quantized-embedding patch and the Humming kernel (GSQ
issue #8: 34-43% of bandwidth on Ampere), so it is neither stock nor fast. RedHatAI is Red Hat's (vLLM
maintainers') llm-compressor release of the official weights, in Marlin's
symmetric g128 layout, and it ships the MTP head (the most-downloaded Int4, cyankiwi's AWQ, is g32 asymmetric
and 21 GB).

Per-step verdict (no MTP, the pure comparison): the GGUF path is faster per step than production's W4A16 at
c=1 (0.95x: 19.6 vs 20.6 ms) and c=2 (0.92x: 20.4 vs 22.3), equal at c=4 (1.00x: 22.7 vs 22.8) and 1.17-1.18x
slower at c=8 (Base 26.7, Swift 26.9 vs 22.8; 1.23x with Swift's first run). It reads 14% fewer bytes per step (11.35 vs 13.25 GB) but streams them 10-27% slower per
byte (580 vs 644 GB/s at c=1, 422 vs 581 at c=8), so the size advantage wins at 1-2 rows and loses by c=8. Against
the stock official INT4 (15.14 GB/step, the most bandwidth-efficient row: 669 GB/s at c=1) the GGUF is 0.87 / 0.84 /
0.92 / 1.08x per step. With MTP the GGUF step (4 rows per sequence) is slower than W4A16's at every c
(Integration 2: 1.01 / 1.16 / 1.19 / 1.07x): an MTP step costs 1.46 / 1.58 / 1.59 / 1.70x a plain step on the
GGUF (Base mtp / Base nomtp, same session and build) vs 1.34 / 1.22 / 1.32 / 1.81x on W4A16 (phase 1b mtp /
today's nomtp, cross-session), i.e. the GGUF's multi-row kernels, not single-row decode, are where
it loses; its tok/s lead with MTP is acceptance (0.63-0.65 vs 0.52).
